# Negotiation and compensation scenarios

Load for negotiation planning or rehearsal, or a comparison requiring timing, living costs or equity scenarios. Use only the parts relevant to the request.

## Plan an actual conversation

Establish the stage and counterpart: an early recruiter salary question, a package discussion, a written offer or a revised offer. Separate the candidate's desired outcome, privately stated minimum and real alternative if no agreement is reached. Missing values can remain unknown. A private constraint is planning context, not permission to disclose it in a draft.

Identify the few terms that matter: role/level, base, guaranteed pay, sign-on, equity, review timing, start date, relocation, flexibility or other stated priorities. Use evidence relevant to the request, such as responsibilities, confirmed experience, current market sources and actual alternatives. Avoid invented leverage, unsupported entitlement and guarantees that a tactic will succeed.

Build a specific ask, its rationale and a useful fallback. Distinguish a request for information from a counteroffer or commitment. Preserve the actual response deadline; an extension can be requested but is not granted until confirmed. Consider which clarification is needed before proposing numbers, and whether the candidate prefers to ask for the employer's range first.

For rehearsal, play the counterpart one turn at a time unless the user requests a full script. Let the candidate respond, then identify what was clear, what was unsupported and what they inadvertently revealed or committed to. Try a different response or objection and let them retry. Label simulated employer reactions as hypothetical. An effective rehearsal does not predict how the actual employer will respond.

## Compare time periods honestly

Choose the comparison horizon and currency. For 12- or 24-month scenarios, use a common reference date and show differences in actual start dates. Distinguish a comparable full employment year from cash received during a particular calendar period.

Record each input's source, date, units, status and payment timing. Useful calculations, when inputs support them, include:

- Fixed cash received over the horizon = payable base during employed periods + guaranteed payments due in the horizon + applicable one-time payments. Show repayment exposure separately.
- Variable cash = a separately labelled payout assumption applied to the actual bonus or commission terms, eligibility and payment dates. Targets are not guarantees.
- Known cost difference = documented relocation costs + housing and other user-selected recurring cost differences over the same period, minus employer reimbursements actually expected under their terms. Show reimbursable costs and reimbursement timing separately when cash timing matters.
- Cash after estimated costs = cash receipts minus selected costs; label it as gross before taxes unless candidate-specific tax inputs are available and verified. It is not a complete savings forecast when expenses are missing.

Do not mechanically double year-one pay for a 24-month result: one-time payments, vesting cliffs, raises, benefits and repayment conditions may differ. Avoid counting a housing allowance both as income and a second reduction of housing costs. Do not include a relocation reimbursement without its associated cost when comparing net relocation effects.

For equity, identify the instrument, grant units or value basis, vesting schedule, exercise/strike price where applicable, expiry and liquidity restrictions. Separate vested units from realizable cash. Show downside/base/upside assumptions and their source; private-company valuation does not promise a sale price. Do not add speculative equity values to money available for rent or relocation.

Show explicit unknowns and conditional ranges instead of invented precision. Verify arithmetic. When priorities or assumptions drive a ranking, vary the ones that could realistically reverse it. Do not require a detailed financial model for a simple salary reply.
