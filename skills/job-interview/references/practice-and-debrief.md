# Practice and debrief guide

Load for an interactive session or a debrief that should inform the next attempt. Store completed notes only in the candidate's private workspace.

## Run a useful session

Identify the target task, available time, assistance mode and evidence of success. Use the employer's evaluation criteria when supplied; mark your own rubric as inferred. A small rubric might assess problem framing, reasoning, correctness, tradeoffs and communication, but adapt it to the actual role.

For an independent attempt, present one task and let the candidate respond. Avoid feeding a polished answer before evaluating their work. Probe missing reasoning without silently turning the attempt into an assisted one. If the candidate requests hints, provide them and record the assistance. For an assisted lesson, explain the concept and work an example; later test transfer with a fresh prompt.

Tie feedback to an observed action or statement. Separate correctness, communication and unfamiliarity so the next exercise addresses the real gap. Do not turn an AI-generated score into a hiring prediction. End at the agreed time or useful stopping point with one concrete retry.

## Capture an interview debrief

Ask for recollections while fresh, respecting the employer's confidentiality rules. Do not reconstruct prohibited live assessment content. Topic-level notes can be enough to guide learning.

Keep observed gaps distinct from explanations for an employer's decision. A rejection without stated reasons does not prove a particular answer caused it. An invitation or scheduled date does not prove the round was completed.

## Propose reusable learning

When repeated evidence suggests a workflow improvement, present the proposed change, supporting observations, scope and affected source. Let the candidate review it before updating standing instructions or skills. Ordinary authorized notes and the next practice plan can be updated directly; they do not require rewriting the public skill or making a permanent rule.
