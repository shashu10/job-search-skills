# Find referral and hiring-post routes

Use for “Find referrals for all the jobs I've applied to” or “Find people promoting this role on LinkedIn or X.” Return useful routes with honest source coverage. For a single role, use its matching records; a full cohort sweep is unnecessary. Do not turn research into a bulk messaging campaign.

## Establish the cohort

Read the mapped application tracker or requested trackers and outreach record. Use the requested time range, locations or role selection; when “all my applications” spans mapped lanes, read-only aggregation does not transfer their queues or action permissions. Deduplicate by employer and exact requisition or verified canonical role URL, retaining source links. Do not infer a submission from a draft, resume or prepared packet.

Account for every role in scope. Prioritize active applications without a useful contact route. Identify existing conversations, recruiter ownership, referral holds, prior requests, user passes and closed/withdrawn processes. Report those needing no new approach with the reason instead of silently dropping them. Do not reopen closed processes or bypass an active recruiter merely to supply a new contact for each row.

## Search the strongest available relationships

Honor explicit source choices. Otherwise work from known relationships toward public discovery:

1. Confirmed personal contacts, former coworkers, alumni and existing conversations in the authorized private workspace or connected sources. Match current employer/team and relevant relationship evidence; resolve ambiguous identities before recommending a person.
2. Existing network connections and past recruiter conversations, using available authorized tools, sessions or user-provided exports. A first-degree connection proves platform connection status, not acquaintance, willingness to refer or hiring authority. An old affiliation needs a current check.
3. Relevant public people and hiring posts on LinkedIn, X, employer sites and appropriate communities. Prefer demonstrated relevance to the role/team/location over a prominent title or large follower count. Public discovery is not a claim to a warm introduction.

Do not require every platform if a useful route is already established. Record what was searched, blocked or unavailable. Missing access to private connections means that source is unchecked, not that no connection exists. Do not create accounts, export an entire address book, purchase contact information or upload private contact lists as a side effect of this research. Read the minimum relevant contact details through available authorized access.

## Find people promoting the opening

Search LinkedIn and X (x.com) using the listing URL/requisition, title, company, team and location, alongside hiring, team-growth or new-office language. Inspect original posts, author affiliation, dates and linked vacancies; distinguish the exact role from a similar opening or general expansion. Save post links with the relevant role/contact evidence.

Recommend a public reply, DM or existing conversation based on the post's invitation, available channel and recorded route. Draft a specific response to what the person posted, honoring their requested contact method. Keep private candidate details out of public replies. An invitation to contact the author does not replace the user's sending authorization.

## Choose a route after submission

Check the exact role's existing application and the employer's evidenced referral process. Some processes permit a referral to an existing application; others require a different route. If feasibility is unknown, suggest asking the relevant contact whether they can support the existing application. Do not promise a referral will attach retroactively.

An introduction, recruiter follow-up, context about the team or hiring-manager conversation may be more useful than a formal referral. State the purpose accurately. Do not withdraw or resubmit an application, create another account, or ask a contact to bypass a recorded hold merely to obtain referral credit. Group related roles under one appropriate conversation when useful, while keeping exact-role evidence separate.

## Deliver a reviewable plan

For each role, return its current stage, recommended route, contact/source, relationship evidence, relevance, post-submission feasibility, existing conversation or hold, and next request. A compact table plus drafts for the strongest routes is usually enough. Avoid a contact dump; show uncertainty and explain when waiting or using an existing route is better.

Keep research and drafts in the mapped private outreach record with role links. Mark drafts **not sent**. Sending requires the applicable recipient/purpose/channel grant from the main skill; research permission does not supply one. Preserve already valid grants without adding another approval ceremony. Record actual sends, replies and referral evidence immediately; application stages remain in their owning trackers.
